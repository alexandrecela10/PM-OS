Opportunity 1

Claims-defence & entitlement-recovery agent

Job to be done (owner-side): contractors claim up to us and routinely for ~2× what they settle for — so defend against inflated claims, catch where the contractor missed its own notice deadline, and rigorously pursue our own liquidated damages and back-charges, off a record we control. (The mirror-image contractor job — recovering under-claimed entitlement — sits in the contractors section at the end.)

Owner Contracts / Commercial Manager

defends against claims, recovers LDs & back-chargesOwner · target

Their job: assess incoming contractor claims fast, rebut the unsupported ones, and pursue the owner's own entitlements before deadlines lapse.

Institutional · IPA · the owner-side proof
The difference between the amount submitted for a claim versus the amount actually paid at settlement… is as much as half. … The owner too must ensure profitability of his project by defending unsupportable claims by a "low ball" contractor.

IPA (Independent Project Analysis) via National Academies · nationalacademies.org · & Long International · long-intl.com

Impact: contractors claim ~2× what they settle for. The owner's win is defending against that gap and recovering its own LDs/back-charges — quantified, unambiguously owner-side.

Institutional · claims advisory
What most projects lose to poor claims management is not a dispute they should have won, but entitlement they never assembled the evidence to claim.

Kairos, Construction Claims Management, 2026 · wearekairos.com

The core failure mode named exactly — money lost for want of evidence assembled in time. Cuts both ways: owners lose defensible positions the same way.

Institutional · practitioner training body
Failure to send notices in the correct form is one of the biggest mistakes that the parties to the FIDIC contracts make.

Institute of Construction Claims Practitioners, FIDIC notices webinar (8,145 views) · via lawinsider.com

Confirms the pain is procedural and universal — and cuts both ways: the agent flags where the contractor missed its deadline, handing the owner a defence.

Cost Engineer / Estimator

builds the estimate, owns the contingencyOwner · target

Their job: produce an estimate that survives contact with reality — and defend the contingency.

Grassroots · project-management course material
All too often it is concluded by senior management that the contingency estimate itself must be wrong. Clearly, they reason, the planners have put in excessive contingencies by making pessimistic assumptions that the problems on the last project will happen again.

Penn State World Campus, Project Budgeting (MANGT 515) · psu.edu

The estimator's lived pain: realistic numbers stripped out as "pessimistic" — the bias Opportunity 2 makes defensible with data.

Institutional · NASA cost-analysis paper
Project planners and advocates often deliberately underestimate costs to help gain project approval… A better method is to base the estimate on the actual costs of a large group of similar projects.

NASA Technical Reports Server, Underestimation of Project Costs · ntrs.nasa.gov

Independently prescribes Opportunity 2's exact method (reference-class forecasting) as the cure.

Opportunity G

Design-feasibility gate

Job to be done: on every design revision during FEED, check in minutes that it's compliant (vs the building code), within cost, and buildable — so scope errors are caught while they cost thousands, not the tens of millions they become in the field.

~5%

70–80%

10× / 100×
cheaper to fix an error in design vs construction vs post-handover

Project Director / Owner (giga-project)

Institutional · independent ROI modelling
An RFI resolved at drawing stage costs $200 to $500 in review time; the same RFI resolved in the field costs $2,000 to $8,000… AI plan review investment creates a cost-avoidance ROI of 4:1 to 30:1.

Mirage Metrics, AI plan-review analysis, 2026 · miragemetrics.com

Impact sizing for an Aramco-scale owner (Opportunity G). On a $5bn giga-project, ~5% rework ≈ $250M, of which ~28% (~$70M) is design-caused; catching half of that early avoids ~$35M on a single project — before counting schedule/first-oil revenue. On a $20bn project the design-caused rework figure is ~$280M. A value-based price of 0.05–0.1% of project value ($2.5M–$5M on $5bn) is trivial against that. Contractors — the secondary use case

But EPC contractors feel the same pains acutely, buy faster (quarters, not years) and per-project, and can be a useful early wedge or channel. The evidence below shows the appetite and willingness to pay on the contractor side — worth keeping warm.
