# Product PRD: FID Approval Red Team

**Product family:** Asset Nova
**Stage:** Not now, here's what has to be true first
**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Buyer:** Gulf owner-operator at Aramco/ADNOC scale
**Primary persona:** Project director, lead estimator and investment committee
**Status:** Deferred until Products 1 and 2 earn trust

## Problem in plain English

The approval vote commits billions against a number that can be too hopeful. EY finds a 23% average overrun after approval. On a $5bn approval, that is $1.15bn of exposure. The project director, lead estimator and investment committee need a challenge that compares the proposal with how comparable projects actually turned out and exposes weak scope, schedule and productivity assumptions before the board commits.

This product is not now. It needs the owner's own historical outcomes, and it delivers an unwelcome message at the highest-stakes moment. Products 1 and 2 must first earn trust and build the owner's project record.

## Persona and their week

The project director is accountable for the budget the board votes on. The lead estimator builds the estimate and owns the contingency. The investment committee decides whether the project number is realistic. Their approval week is spent reviewing the design package, cost and schedule, comparing assumptions, challenging optimism, and preparing the gate meeting. Once the vote happens, every later overrun is measured against it.

## Job to be done

Before the board commits billions, pressure-test the number against how comparable past projects actually turned out, and surface where the estimate is optimistic or the design under-defined, so the project is sanctioned on a realistic number, not a hopeful one.

## Value decomposition

### Value (money)

EY finds a 23% average overrun **after** approval. On a $5bn approval, that's $1.15bn of exposure. Taking a fifth of it off the table is **$230M**.

### Likelihood of delivering

**Medium.** The product needs the owner's own project history and comparable outcomes. It also needs to make a difficult message credible to the most senior people in the room.

### Effort from the persona

**High.** The owner must hand over portfolio history and help organise it into comparable projects, outcomes and assumptions.

### Sacrifice from the persona

**Political.** The product may say that the number presented for approval is wrong. It challenges the biggest vote rather than making the approval process more comfortable.

## What has to be true first

- Claims Defence & Recovery has run on closed and live records with cited findings.
- Design Feasibility has shown that narrow, cited checks can prevent expensive field changes.
- The owner has a usable history of project outcomes, or agrees to a paid history clean-up step.
- A project director, estimator and investment committee agree what evidence can challenge the estimate.
- The first retrospective demo can show what the product would have flagged at a past approval.

## Deferred scope

When those conditions are true, v1 can:

- Compare a proposal with comparable past projects and outcomes.
- Audit the design package for gaps that predict overruns.
- Test productivity, budget and schedule assumptions against the owner's history.
- Produce a cited challenge book for the gate meeting.
- Record which challenges were accepted, rejected or deferred for later learning.

### Explicit non-goals for now

- No live FID sale before Products 1 and 2 earn trust.
- No claim that public data alone replaces the owner's portfolio history.
- No autonomous approval recommendation or board action.
- No invented benchmark or untraceable comparison.

## User journeys

### Primary journey: approval challenge book

1. Tom Beckett, project director for Jebel Nasr, brings Train 5 to the investment committee with a $6.1bn estimate and 8% contingency. Layla Haddad, lead estimator for capital projects, owns the estimate.
2. Before forward use, the owner gives the product the history of 14 finished owner projects. The product compares the Train 5 assumptions and audits under-defined scope against that reference class.
3. It produces a cited challenge book of nine questions, including what is missing from the definition at this gate and how the 8% contingency compares with the 23% median historical growth.
4. Layla receives the private pre-read first and can respond before Tom takes the questions to the gate meeting.
5. The investment committee accepts, edits or rejects each question before the vote. The agent asks questions and casts no vote.
6. The gate decision and later outcome are stored for the next approval.

### Alternate 1: retrospective demo

Before any sale, run the product on the last approval and show the questions it would have raised. Use this only as a trust-building demonstration, not as a promise that the next vote is safe.

### Alternate 2: history clean-up

Start with a paid step that organises closed-project history into comparable outcomes. Until the owner's history exists, use public outcome data only as a clearly labelled interim input.

## Success metrics

### Business

- Share of approval exposure addressed, with the $230M example as the value frame.
- Number and materiality of challenges accepted before the vote.
- Later overrun versus the approved number.

### Product

- Share of challenge-book findings reviewed by the project director and estimator.
- Share of findings accepted, edited or rejected with a recorded reason.
- Time to prepare the gate challenge book.
- Reuse of the historical record at the next approval.

### AI

- Recall against the specialist's retrospective findings on closed approvals.
- Zero tolerance for invented facts in cited challenges.
- Accuracy of comparisons to the named comparable projects.
- Cost per document read.

All targets need validation after Products 1 and 2 have produced the owner history and the first retrospective demo.

## Adoption rules

1. **Runs on files they already have.** Read-only. No migration, no new system of record.
2. **No new habit.** It arrives where they already look (email or Teams), and reviews happen on one screen.
3. **The agent never sends.** It drafts. A person presses send. Always.
4. **Every claim is clickable.** No assertion without a citation to a page in a real document.
5. **Proof on history first.** We run on last year's closed files and show what it would have caught, before it touches a live project.
6. **Priced against one avoided loss,** and sized so a pilot sits inside one project team's own authority.

## Risks

| Risk | Response |
|---|---|
| The owner lacks organised history | Make history clean-up the first paid step and label public data as interim |
| The product is politically rejected | Earn the right through Products 1 and 2, then start with a retrospective demo |
| A comparison is not genuinely comparable | Require named source projects and a human review gate |
| The message is treated as a board decision | Keep the product advisory and make the investment committee the decision owner |
