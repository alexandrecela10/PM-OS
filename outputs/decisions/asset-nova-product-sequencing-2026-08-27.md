# Decision: what Asset Nova sells, and in what order

**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Owner:** Alexandre Cela
**Status:** Decided for the round 2 case study, open to challenge on the pricing and the $400M assumption

## Decision

Sell three products off one reading layer, in this order.

1. **Claims defence and recovery.** Always on during build. First product, first pilot, first revenue.
2. **Design feasibility.** Event driven during design and FEED. Sold alongside or straight after Product 1.
3. **FID red teaming.** Once per approval gate. Enabled only after Products 1 and 2 have earned trust.

## Why this order

- Product 1 is the only one whose value lands inside a single project team's own budget authority, on a loss they can name. It also runs on documents the owner already controls, so it needs no data-sharing deal with a contractor.
- Product 2 needs a code edition and a budget ceiling to check against, which is a five-minute setup, and it produces labelled disputes that make the whole reading layer smarter.
- Product 3 tells the most senior people in the room that their number is optimistic, and it needs the owner's historical project outcomes. Both of those are things a trusted supplier gets and a new one does not. Selling it first would be selling an argument.

## What we are not doing

- No writing back into ERP, P6 or Aconex in v1.
- No autonomous outbound correspondence, in any product, ever. The agent drafts, a named person sends.
- No contractor-side version. The buyer is the owner.
- No product that blocks a design revision.
- No request that a live project team change its monthly reporting cycle.

## What this decision rests on

Confirmed by research: the scale of overrun and dispute (64% over budget and 59% average cost growth in oil and gas, 89% over cost in the Middle East, 33.4% of contract budgets in dispute, $60.1M and about 12.5 months per average US dispute), the claim-versus-settlement gap of as much as half, design as the origin of 70 to 80% of cost deviations, and Gulf willingness to buy at scale after a proof of concept ($340M ADNOC agentic AI contract).

Assumed and still to validate: that claims submitted over a $5bn build run at about 8% of project value, that sharper defence keeps about 10% of what would have been paid, that recovery moves from about 40% to about 70% of what is owed, and that 0.05% of project value is the right pilot price.

## Next checks

- Validate the 8% claims-submission assumption against one owner's real portfolio before the number goes in front of a buyer.
- Run Product 1 on one closed project archive. Pass mark: it finds what the commercial team found, plus at least one material item they missed, every claim cited.
- Run Product 2 across one FEED revision history. Pass mark: it flags the errors that became field changes.
- Agree the money-kept metric in writing with the first pilot customer before the pilot starts.
